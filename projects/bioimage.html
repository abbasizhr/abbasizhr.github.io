Featured Project

Computational Bioimage Analysis for Quantitative Measurement

A workflow for measuring objects in light-microscopy images: load, preprocess, segment, measure each object, evaluate against instance-level ground truth, and then quantify how far those measurements can still be trusted once the image quality drops.

Deliberately classical, with no deep learning anywhere. The aim was never leaderboard performance — it was a pipeline transparent enough that every step can be inspected and every number traced back to a parameter written down in one file.

The result the project is actually about

Two ways of scoring the same segmentation disagree, and the disagreement is the finding. Read only the pixel-overlap column and the problem looks close to solved. Count the nuclei and the pipeline is short by roughly seven per image.

Pixel-level overlap alone can overstate quantitative measurement reliability, because split and merge errors alter object counts even when the predicted masks have substantial overlap with the reference. Two touching nuclei merged into one blob barely move a Dice score and destroy a count. So results are reported per acquisition modality, with explicit split and merge counters, and with a macro average rather than a single flattering pooled number — the modality groups are badly imbalanced, and a dataset-wide mean hides how far performance drops on the smallest of them.

The dataset, and why only part of it is used

BBBC038v1 — the 2018 Data Science Bowl nuclei collection. Only the stage1_train archive is used, because it is the only part with public instance-level ground truth: one binary mask per nucleus, which is what makes object counting and per-object precision and recall possible at all.

Two properties of the collection drive every later decision. Polarity is mixed: 124 of the 670 images are dark nuclei on a light background, and a naive pipeline silently segments the background on those while still reporting a plausible Dice. So polarity is decided once, recorded in a manifest with a hand-override column, and never re-derived — two places deciding polarity is two places to disagree. And the grouping is done by a readable two-question rule rather than a fitted clustering, so it can be explained in a sentence and corrected by hand.

The 200/470 dev/test split is assigned by a hash of each image's identifier, so it depends on neither file ordering nor a random number generator's version: an image lands in the same split forever. Four parameters were tuned, and tuning and reporting on the same images would have made every number an in-sample estimate.

How the pipeline works

Preprocessing

Drop the constant alpha channel (asserted, not assumed), reduce to one grayscale channel, normalize polarity from the manifest, white-tophat background subtraction, a small median filter, then CLAHE with a deliberately low clip limit — a high one amplifies histology background texture into false objects. Images are never resized, because resizing changes object scale in pixels and would make a minimum-area threshold mean different things on different images.

Marker-controlled watershed

Distance transform, smoothed by one pixel, then h_maxima as markers. The one-pixel smoothing is not cosmetic: the distance transform of a binary blob is full of exactly-tied plateaus, and h_maxima on the raw transform returns one marker per tie group instead of one per nucleus. The depth h is in distance-transform pixels, so it reads directly: two nuclei separate when the neck between them is at least h pixels narrower in radius than the smaller one.

Instance-level metrics

IoU between every ground-truth and predicted object, built in one vectorized pass over a contingency table — at this scale that is the difference between seconds and hours. Matching is greedy and one-to-one. Above an IoU of 0.5 that is not an approximation: two disjoint predictions each overlapping more than half of the same nucleus would together need more area than the nucleus has, so the pairs above the threshold are already a one-to-one matching. The comparison is strict (> rather than ≥), matching the published DSB definition and disposing of the tie case at exactly 0.5.

Verification without a test suite

No pytest, by design. The two plausible failures here — polarity inversion and watershed split/merge — are both silent: they produce a believable Dice while the result is wrong. So the checks live where they are visible. Every run begins by scoring four synthetic cases with known answers and printing them. The split-nucleus case scores Dice 1.00 with F1@0.5 of 0.00, which is the whole argument of the project shown rather than asserted.

In this dataset the brightfield images gave substantially lower segmentation performance than either fluorescence group, and the shortfall looks structural rather than statistical: these brightfield nuclei have low contrast against cytoplasm and soft boundaries, so a global threshold has no clean level to find, and the distance transform inside a poorly-delimited blob offers the watershed no reliable peak to use as a marker. The result is specific to this collection and should not be read as a property of brightfield microscopy in general.

On the published baselines. The dataset ships a leaderboard, and these metrics use the same definitions, so the numbers can be put side by side — but they are not like-for-like and the comparison has to be read with that in front of it. The pooled F1@0.5 of 0.784 appears to exceed a published U-Net entry at 0.734. That is not a win and is not claimed as one: the published rows score the competition's held-out test sets, which were assembled to include acquisition types absent from stage1_train, while this split is a partition of stage1_train itself and is dominated by the easiest category in the collection. On the official DSB score, which penalizes at every IoU threshold from 0.5 to 0.95, this work sits below every published entry by a wide margin — which is the expected and correct position for a transparent classical baseline.

How far can the measurements be trusted?

Images are degraded along four controlled axes and the whole pipeline is re-run on each one, so “the method is sensitive to image quality” becomes a measurement with a shape rather than an assertion.

Noise. Grayscale fluorescence holds up to about σ = 0.05 and then falls away sharply, from F1@0.5 of 0.922 down to 0.467 at σ = 0.2. Brightfield degrades earlier and further (0.452 → 0.082). Colour histology is the most robust (0.760 → 0.674), because stained nuclei carry a large intensity separation from the surrounding tissue.

Blur. Everything degrades. A single σ in pixels is not a fair insult across groups, since nucleus radii span roughly 5 to 30 pixels, so blur is also plotted against σ divided by each image's own median nucleus radius.

Contrast — an exactly flat null result. Across all six levels, down to a compression factor of 0.05, the median F1@0.5 in every group is identical to its baseline; the largest spread within any group is 0.0000. This is not a broken experiment. Otsu's threshold is equivariant under an affine intensity transform and the pipeline works in float32, so contrast compression about the image median has no route by which to change the segmentation — neither clipping nor quantization intervenes.

Contrast × noise — the informative version. Compress the contrast and then add a fixed σ = 0.02 of noise, so the signal shrinks while the noise floor stays put: F1@0.5 falls from 0.705 at full contrast to 0.489 at factor 0.15 and 0.053 at 0.05. The physical quantity governing segmentation is the ratio between signal and noise floor, not contrast itself — which is exactly what the flat result above was pointing at.

What the study found

  1. Pixel overlap and object counting disagree, and only one of them is measuring the real failure. Pooled Dice 0.872 against per-object F1@0.5 of 0.784, with 1,814 merge events — a shortfall of about seven nuclei per image. Pixel overlap alone would have overstated how reliable the object counts are.
  2. The macro average over modalities is the honest aggregate. It falls to Dice 0.781 and F1@0.5 0.641, because a pooled mean is dominated by the 546 easiest images and hides the marked performance degradation on the 21 hardest.
  3. In this dataset, brightfield performed substantially worse than the fluorescence modalities, structurally rather than statistically. Low contrast against cytoplasm leaves a global threshold no clean level to find and the watershed no reliable marker.
  4. Contrast compression alone changes nothing at all — a provable consequence of Otsu's affine equivariance, and a useful reminder that a flat curve can be a result rather than a bug.
  5. What actually governs segmentation is signal against the noise floor. Compress contrast and add fixed noise, and F1@0.5 collapses from 0.705 to 0.053.
  6. The variation is between images, not between noise draws. The spread across three noise realizations of one image is 6–14% of the spread across images, so the reported uncertainty is about the collection rather than about the random seed.

Limitations. Only stage1_train is used, so the split is easier than the competition's held-out sets and the comparison against published entries is indicative rather than like-for-like. The approach is classical throughout, and on the strict DSB score it sits below every published learned method — which is the point of a baseline, not a shortcoming of the experiment. The colour-fluorescence group admitted by the grouping rule has no members in this archive, so that cell of the design is untested.
